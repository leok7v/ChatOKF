What people actually use it for

It answers much like the assistants you have used in a browser. The difference is where the answer is computed.

  • Ask anything

    Untangle a lease clause, plan a week of dinners, check the math on a loan.

  • Drop in a document

    A PDF, a Word file or a spreadsheet. Ask which line grew fastest and it reads the table to answer.

  • Show it a picture

    A photo of a whiteboard, a menu or a street sign. Some models also listen to audio and watch video.

  • Watch it think

    The stronger models reason before they answer. Read it, cut it short, or switch thinking off for speed.

  • Let it look things up

    Wikipedia, today's headlines, the weather and web search, each behind its own switch.

  • Talk instead of typing

    Tap the microphone and speak, and have replies read aloud in one of ten voices.

It looks things up.

Ask about something it cannot know and it searches Wikipedia or the web, shows you each step, and answers from what it read.

Where your words go

Nowhere, unless it looks something up.

Most assistants send your sentence to a company. ChatᵒᵏF has nowhere to send it: the whole model sits in your Mac's memory and runs on its own chip. Privacy here is not a policy that could be revised next year. It is the shape of the thing.

Wikipedia and web search are the one time anything leaves. For Wikipedia the matching happens on your Mac first, so what travels is the article it decided to open, not the question you asked. For the web, the search terms the model writes go to a search service. One button, airplane mode, switches both off.

No account. No sign-in. No subscription. No ads, no analytics, no telemetry.

It remembers, in notes you can read

Tell it to remember something and it writes a short note, and the chat says what it wrote. Later questions are checked against your notes first, whichever model is answering.

Every note is listed under Memories, to read or to delete. They are plain text files on your Mac, so switching models keeps everything the app has learned. One switch, Total Recall, turns all of it off.

Pick a model, or keep the one it starts with

Several open models are offered, and the list shows only the ones your Mac can actually hold, so every one you see will run. A small one answers almost instantly; a large one is noticeably smarter and wants a Mac with memory to spare.

Gemma 4 E2B, E4B Quick, and they see pictures, hear sound and watch video.
Gemma 4 12B The same senses with more depth. 16 GB of memory and up.
Qwen3.5 4B, 9B The everyday range, good with tools and long answers.
Qwen3.8 27B The most thoughtful, in a size for each Mac.
Ternary Bonsai 1.7B, 27B Answer directly, without reasoning first.

Each model is downloaded once, its integrity checked, and then works offline. On a MacBook Air, Gemma 4 E4B writes about 27 words a second; the numbers for every model are on GitHub.

Prefer the command line?

Homebrew installs the same signed and notarised build.

brew tap leok7v/tap
brew trust leok7v/tap
brew install --cask chatokf

The middle line is Homebrew's own gate on taps that are not its own; it asks for it by name if you skip it. The cask lives in a separate repository because brew tap clones a whole repo, and this one carries a 132 MB model file.

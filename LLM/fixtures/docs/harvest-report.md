# Northwind Orchards

## Harvest Report, Third Quarter

Rain arrived late in the valley this year, and the trees answered the way
they always do when the ground stays wet into June: heavy fruit on the
lower branches and almost nothing above the reach of a short ladder. The
pickers finished the eastern blocks a full week behind schedule and made
none of it back on the western slope, where the fog sat until midmorning
most days and the crews could not start until the leaves had dried.

The cider press ran a second shift through most of August. That was not
planned, but the windfall from the storm on the eleventh was too large to
sort by hand and too bruised to sell whole, so it went to the press
instead of the packing house. The result reads oddly in the figures
below: a poor season by weight, and the best cider yield the orchard has
recorded.

| Block    | Trees | Tonnes | Yield per tree | To press |
| -------- | ----- | ------ | -------------- | -------- |
| Eastern  |   840 |   47.2 |         56.2kg |      18% |
| Western  |   610 |   22.9 |         37.5kg |      41% |
| Riverbank|   295 |   19.4 |         65.8kg |       9% |
| Nursery  |   150 |    3.1 |         20.7kg |       0% |

Riverbank remains the puzzle. It carries the fewest trees of any mature
block and returns the most fruit from each of them, on ground the survey
still lists as marginal. The agronomist has been asked twice for an
explanation and has twice suggested drainage, which is almost certainly
correct and almost certainly incomplete.

Two decisions follow from the season. The nursery block will not be
picked commercially again until the fourth year, since three tonnes does
not pay for the labour to gather it. And the western slope will be
thinned over the winter, hard, on the theory that fewer trees standing
further apart will dry sooner in the morning and give the crews back the
week they lost.
